RSNA Knee Project Map

Only 58 of 4,407 training studies have real labels. So an LLM reads the other studies' radiology reports and turns them into soft labels that copy the annotators' image-based rules, checked against a held-out half of the gold set. A 2.5D CNN then learns those labels from the fat-suppressed and anatomy views, with folds grouped by hospital, because the test set likely contains hospitals the training set never saw.

1 · What the data looks like

done

A study is one knee exam. It holds about 5 series (scans from different directions and settings), and each series is a stack of about 30 slices. The test set has images only. The reports exist only for training.

  • Only 58 studies carry real labels, and they were picked to contain findings: every one has at least one positive, and 31% have a fracture.
  • Gold labels sometimes contradict the reports: a report says "moderate effusion" and the gold label says 0. The host confirmed the labels were read from the images with strict thresholds.
  • In training, the two sequence flags are always equal. "0/0" means no fat suppression (T1, PD or T2), not T1.
  • Reports come in 9 languages. The gold set has no French report.

2 · Validation design

done · folds frozen

There are two yardsticks. Cross-validation folds over the 4,349 pseudo-labelled studies are large and stable, which makes them good for comparing models. The 58 gold studies are small but true, which makes them good for an honest final check.

Folds keep each hospital whole. The data has no hospital column, so the code infers one from the report language (where only one hospital uses that language) or from the report's fixed opening template.

3 · Report labeller

done

The LLM gets the official label definitions and returns, for each of the 12 findings, one of five states plus a short evidence quote. A fixed mapping then turns each state into a probability, which becomes the training target.

Version 1 used a single "negative" state and scored 0.788 on gold dev. Splitting it into "below threshold" (for example "small effusion") and "absent" raised that to 0.880, because AUC rewards ranking and the mild mentions carry ranking information. Version 3 changes no LLM calls: when a report is silent on synovitis, the label borrows from effusion, which radiologists report far more readily.

4 · DICOM preprocessing

done on Kaggle

Each study becomes one fixed-size array: 4 view slots × 24 slices × 256 × 256 pixels, plus a flag per slot saying whether that view exists. The same code runs on Kaggle for training and inside the submission notebook for the test set, so the model never sees images prepared differently.

  • Slices are ordered by physical position from the DICOM header, because the file names are random.
  • Each series is cropped to the body: a 3D axial scan is 640 × 1280 pixels with the knee small in the middle.
  • Intensities are rescaled per series, because MRI values have no fixed units across scanners.

5 · KneeNet and training

baseline done · LB 0.916

One ImageNet-pretrained 2D network looks at every slice of every view. Each slice is fed with its two neighbours as the three colour channels, which gives a little 3D context. Per view, learned attention picks out the slices that matter. One transformer layer lets the views exchange information. Views that are missing are masked out, and the result feeds 12 outputs.

Training uses binary cross-entropy against the soft labels. Augmentation includes left–right flips (safe, because medial stays medial on a mirrored knee) and randomly hiding whole views, so the model copes with studies that lack a view.

What to defend in an interview

Decisions

Each entry gives the choice, why, the evidence, and what would change it. Open one to read it.

D1The 58 gold studies are for evaluation only58 extra training studies add 1.3%; an honest yardstick is worth more.

Evidence: they are enriched (every one has ≥1 positive, Fracture 31%), so their rates say nothing about the test set.

Would change if: a larger gold set appeared.

D2Training targets come from reports, read by an LLMIt is the only label source for 4,349 studies, and it handles negation, severity and 9 languages.

Evidence: the host explicitly allows LLM APIs. Forum benchmark against the 58: keyword rules 0.814, LLM 0.878.

Would change if: LLM labels scored no better than rules on gold dev.

D3The labeller copies the annotators' definitions, and "not addressed" is its own answerGold labels are image-based with strict thresholds; silence means different things per finding.

Evidence: host confirmed image-based, authoritative labels. In our gold dev data, silence was 0% positive for Baker's cyst but 22% for synovitis.

D4Soft labels with binary cross-entropyBCE is minimised at p = y, so a target of 0.3 teaches "30% likely".

Hard 0/1 labels would inject confident errors wherever a report is unclear.

D5Gold split: dev 29 for tuning, test 29 scored onceTuning on the studies you score on makes the score optimistic.

Multilabel-stratified halves, seed 0, 4–18 positives per label per half. With 29 studies, AUC gaps under about 0.02–0.03 are noise.

D65 folds, grouped by hospital, balanced over all 12 labelsValidation must look like the test, and the test likely has unseen hospitals.

Evidence: hospitals in Italy and Mongolia are credited, yet no training report is in Italian or Mongolian.

How: greedy assignment, largest hospital first, each to the fold where it adds the least imbalance. No library does grouped and multilabel together.

D7Inputs are view slots chosen by plane and fat suppressionEach finding shows best in particular views, and the flags are given at test time.

MCL reads on coronal, ACL on sagittal, bone bruises on fat-suppressed views.

D82.5D, not 3DVoxels are 0.16 mm in-plane but about 3.3 mm between slices; 2D encoders come pretrained.

A 3D network would be heavier, pretrained on less data, and would spend its capacity on the coarse axis.

D9Preprocess on Kaggle with the exact inference codeThe 570 GB stays put, and train and test images are prepared identically.

Only 15 GB of arrays moves to the GPU cluster.

D10A modest encoder; effort goes into labelsForum: a bigger encoder gained +0.0011; labels are the binding constraint.

Encoder size is one of the planned M4 experiments, after labels and views.

D11Walking skeleton firstAn early end-to-end submission finds packaging and runtime problems while there is time.

The first leaderboard score is targeted for the day training starts.

D12OpenAI gpt-6.1-sol, low reasoning, Batch APIStrong mid-tier model, half price in batch, key already available.

Gold dev cost $0.13 at standard price, about 350 output tokens per report. One request format serves both sync and batch, the output is strict JSON, and every answer is cached on disk.

D13Keep a "below threshold" severity tierAUC rewards ranking; "small effusion" ranks above "no effusion" even though both are 0.

Evidence (gold dev): v1 0.788 → v2 0.880. In v1, "negative" effusions were 36% gold-positive, and silence ranked wrongly above "negative" for 4 labels.

D14Four slots; missing views are maskedFat-sat views exist in 94–100% of studies; sagittal non-fat-sat in 97%.

Slot type uses the fluid-sensitive flag, so unseen flag combinations still map somewhere. Duplicates keep the series with the most slices. A test proves a missing view's pixels never change the output.

D1524 slices, crop to body, 256 px, per-series scalingFixed shape for batching; no wasted field of view; scanner-independent intensities.

Percentile scaling uses the 1st–99.5th percentiles mapped to 0–255.

D16Shared encoder, attention over slices, fusion over viewsA finding sits in a few slices of one or two views.

2.5D neighbour channels, slot-type embedding, one transformer layer across views, masked mean, 12-output head.

D17Training recipeStandard and stable choices; the experiments go elsewhere.

AdamW 3e-4, warm-up then cosine, bf16, gradient clip 1, 12 epochs. Flips are label-safe because the labels are anatomical. View dropout matches test studies with missing views.

D18Kaggle preprocesses, a GPU cluster trains, Kaggle submitsMove 15 GB instead of 570 GB, and train on A100s.

Three Kaggle CPU notebooks run in parallel, one shard each.

D19Labelling spend is capped and never paid twice$19 of credit, hard stop at $15.

OpenAI's Tier-1 queue holds 900k tokens for this model, so the reports go through in sequential chunks of 350. A restart first collects batches already in flight.

D20Silent synovitis borrows from effusionRadiologists report effusion readily and synovitis rarely, and the two travel together.

How: only when the report says nothing about synovitis: p = 0.05 + 0.6 × p(effusion). Explicit statements are never overridden.

Evidence: forum: P(synovitis | effusion) 0.63 vs 0.22. Gold dev: Synovitis 0.745 → 0.806, macro 0.880 → 0.885. The rule was decided before looking, and the macro gain alone is within noise.

D21Self-training: report labels blended with out-of-fold image predictionsThe test is scored on image-based labels; a model that has seen thousands of knees corrects what reports miss.

How: new label = 0.5 × LLM label + 0.5 × prediction from the one fold model that never trained on that study. Gold studies are never relabelled.

Evidence (gold dev): labels alone 0.885, predictions alone 0.916, 50/50 mix 0.933. Student fold 0 at epoch 6: gold 0.899 against 0.875 for its teacher, matching the whole 5-fold teacher ensemble.

D22A second architecture for ensemble diversityTwo different models make different mistakes; averaging them helps more than another fold of the same model.

ResNet-50 with plain mean pooling: fold 0 gold 0.878 (B0: 0.870), predictions correlate 0.857 with B0. B0 + ResNet-50 on fold 0 averaged 0.892, above two B0 folds (0.885).

Interview

How to explain it

"Only 58 studies had real labels, so I turned 4,349 radiology reports into soft labels with an LLM, tuned to the annotators' image-based definitions and checked on a held-out half of the gold set. Then I trained a 2.5D multi-view CNN with hospital-grouped cross-validation, because the test set likely contains unseen hospitals."
Why not train on the 58 labelled studies?They add 1.3% data but are the only true yardstick, and they are enriched, so their label rates are misleading. (D1)
How do you know the LLM labels are any good?Macro AUC 0.880 on 29 held-out gold studies, against a published keyword baseline of 0.814. The prompt was tuned on dev only; test is scored once. (D5, D13)
What was the biggest single improvement so far?Not a bigger model: splitting "negative" into "below threshold" and "absent". AUC is about ranking, and mild mentions carry it. +0.09 on gold dev. (D13)
Why group folds by hospital?Same-hospital studies in train and validation leak scanner style and case mix. Sites credited in Italy and Mongolia have no training reports, so the test likely has unseen hospitals. (D6)
Why 2.5D instead of a 3D CNN?Slices are 20× coarser than pixels, so the volume is sharp in 2D only. 2D encoders come ImageNet-pretrained; neighbour channels add the little through-plane context. (D8)
What happens when a view is missing?The slot is masked and provably ignored, and training randomly hides views so the model never depends on one. (D14, D17)
